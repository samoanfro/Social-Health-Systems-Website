SHs Credentials

Earned credentials mean something. Ours are earned through demonstrated behavior change, not purchased.

Why Earned Credentials Matter

Most training platforms issue certificates. Certificates mean you attended. SHs credentials mean you demonstrate observable, measurable behavior change against a defined standard. Credentials only matter if they can't be gamed. Every tier requires evidence—observed practice, portfolio documentation, assessed capability, and peer feedback. The credential carries weight because the process to earn it is rigorous and verifiable.

The 5-Tier Pathway

Tier 1 — Entry Level
Social Health Certified
Awareness → First Evidence

What You'll Learn

Precise definition of Social Health. First Connection Currency Tools. Behavioral evidence that deliberate relational work changes how interactions land.

Modules

Modules 1–2 (7 hours total)

Assessment

7-day behavioral log + 30-day commitment naming two specific behavioral changes

Time Commitment

1 live session (3.5 hrs) + 7-day practice

Validity

2 years. Renewal: Refresher session + updated commitment

Entry Credential
Tier 2 — Practitioner
Social Health Practitioner
Toolkit Mastery → Behavior Change

What You'll Learn

Master the Communication Toolkit. Handle hard conversations without triggering threat response. De-escalate conflict. Feedback that lands and trust survives the exchange.

Modules

Modules 3–5 (16 hours total) + prerequisites

Prerequisites

Social Health Certified (Tier 1)

Assessment

2 observed role-plays scored against rubric + 3-deployment workplace log + 10-dimension relational systems map

Time Commitment

3 half-day sessions over 2–3 weeks + 90 days of application

Validity

2 years. Renewal: Refresher + updated application log

Practitioner Credential
Tier 3 — Specialist
Social Health Specialist
Self-Relational Work → Executive Presence

What You'll Learn

Interrupt self-criticism patterns that drive avoidance. Build regulated presence under sustained pressure and high-stakes negotiation. Design peer networks deliberately. Lead with presence.

Modules

Modules 6–7 (24 hours total) + prerequisites

Prerequisites

Social Health Practitioner (Tier 2)

Assessment

60-day portfolio of 5 documented situations + 1 observed coaching conversation + anonymous 360 micro-feedback from 2 direct reports

Time Commitment

4 sessions over 60 days + ongoing portfolio practice

Validity

2 years. Renewal: Updated portfolio + 360 micro-feedback

Senior Credential
Tier 4 — Coach
Social Health Coach
Facilitator Certification → Deliver at Scale

What You'll Learn

Certification to deliver Modules 1–7. Assess Tiers 1–2. Build internal facilitator capacity. Lead scaled implementations across organizations.

Modules

Module 8 (full) + certified delivery of Modules 1–7

Prerequisites

Social Health Specialist (Tier 3) + 2 years active SHs application

Assessment

Live teach-back evaluated by Tier 5 mentor + written enterprise facilitation plan + scope-of-practice scenario response + 1 supervised Tier 1 cohort delivery

Time Commitment

3-day intensive + 90 days of supervised delivery

Validity

3 years. Renewal: Annual calibration session + portfolio of 6+ cohorts delivered

Facilitator Credential
Tier 5 — Master
Social Health Master
Curriculum Stewardship → Framework Authority

What You'll Learn

Authority to evolve curriculum within the standard. Certify Tier 4 coaches. Diagnose enterprise relational health across complex multi-site organizations. Hold clinical and high-intensity facilitation judgment.

Scope

All 9 modules + curriculum stewardship authority. SHs-only credential. By invitation only.

Prerequisites

Social Health Coach (Tier 4) + minimum 2 years active program delivery + exceptional demonstrated judgment

Assessment

3+ Tier 4 coaches certified under supervised oversight + enterprise case study + final review by SHs Standards Governing Body

Time Commitment

6–12 month mentorship under sitting Tier 5 mentor

Validity

Ongoing. Continuous active stewardship. No expiration.

Master Credential

What Makes Credentials Rigorous

Observed Practice

Not self-reported. Facilitators observe role-plays, workplace application, and team impact. What happens in the room matters more than what participants say happened.

Portfolio Evidence

Documented examples of relational capability applied in real contexts. Situations, decisions, outcomes. Peer and team feedback on impact. This is verifiable.

Rubric Calibration

Every tier assesses against the same defined rubric. Consistency of standards across facilitators, organizations, and years. The rubric protects fidelity.
