Building a Sense of Belonging in Schools and Workplaces: Why Connection Shapes Culture

A strong culture is not defined only by who is included. It is defined by whether people feel that they have a meaningful place within it. By Corinne Siaosi, Social Health Systems Intern

6 min read

A strong culture is not defined only by who is included. It is defined by whether people feel that they have a meaningful place within it.

In schools and workplaces, belonging influences how people participate, communicate, and contribute. A student may attend the same school every day but still feel disconnected from the community around them. An employee may complete their responsibilities, attend meetings, and interact with colleagues while feeling detached from the purpose and people connected to their work.

This difference between being present and truly belonging has become an important consideration for organizations.

Inclusion creates access. Belonging creates connection.

A person can be invited into a classroom, workplace, or team without feeling that they are genuinely part of it. Belonging develops when individuals experience respect, recognition, and a sense that their presence has value.

When people feel connected to the environments around them, they are more likely to engage, collaborate, and contribute. When that connection is missing, individuals often begin to distance themselves from the people and communities around them.

Creating belonging requires more than bringing people together. It requires intentional attention to the relationships, behaviors, and experiences that shape how people feel within a culture.

The Difference Between Inclusion and Belonging

Many organizations focus on inclusion by ensuring people have access to opportunities, resources, and participation. These efforts are essential, but inclusion alone does not always create a sense of connection.

Belonging is the deeper experience of feeling accepted and valued within a community.

For students, belonging may come from feeling recognized by teachers, supported by peers, and confident that they have a meaningful role within their school.

For employees, belonging may come from feeling that their ideas are respected, their contributions matter, and their work connects to a larger purpose.

Without belonging, people may participate only at a surface level. They may complete tasks or meet expectations while feeling emotionally separate from the community around them.

This is why two people can share the same environment and have completely different experiences. One person may feel connected and supported, while another may feel overlooked.

The difference is often created through everyday interactions.

Why Belonging Influences Engagement

Engagement is often viewed as a question of motivation, productivity, or personal responsibility. However, engagement is also influenced by how connected people feel to the environment they are part of.

When individuals believe they matter within a group, they are more likely to share ideas, participate in discussions, and invest in collective goals.

In workplaces, belonging can influence collaboration, employee commitment, and retention. People are more likely to remain engaged when they feel connected to their colleagues and understand how their work contributes to something meaningful.

In schools, belonging can affect participation, confidence, and willingness to seek support. Students who feel connected to their school community often have stronger opportunities to engage academically and socially.

Belonging does not remove challenges or expectations. Instead, it creates an environment where people are more willing to face those challenges because they feel supported within the community.

How Cultures Accidentally Create Disconnection

A lack of belonging usually does not develop from one major moment. More often, it grows through repeated experiences that make people feel unseen or disconnected.

In workplaces, employees may begin to feel that they are valued only for their output rather than their ideas, perspective, or contribution to the team. Communication can become focused entirely on tasks, leaving little room for meaningful connection.

In schools, students may feel disconnected when academic performance becomes the only measure of success and opportunities for relationship-building are overlooked.

These patterns often happen unintentionally. Schools and workplaces are complex environments with many competing priorities.

However, when relationships are consistently treated as secondary, belonging becomes harder to establish.

People need more than access to a community. They need experiences that show they are recognized within it.

The Everyday Behaviors That Create Belonging

Belonging is built through repeated interactions.

Large events and organized activities can provide opportunities for connection, but the daily behaviors within a culture determine whether people actually feel valued.

Recognition plays an important role. When people notice and acknowledge each other’s efforts, individuals receive a message that their contributions matter.

Participation also strengthens belonging. People are more connected to environments where they have opportunities to influence, contribute, and be involved in meaningful ways.

Consistency creates another important foundation. When expectations are clear and interactions are respectful, people develop confidence in the environment around them.

Connection grows when individuals experience genuine interest and support rather than only functional interactions.

These behaviors may seem small, but culture is shaped through repeated experiences.

Creating Belonging in Schools

Schools are communities where students develop more than academic knowledge. They also learn how to communicate, build relationships, manage challenges, and understand their role within a larger group.

A strong sense of belonging can influence how students approach learning and relationships. Students who feel connected are often more willing to participate, ask questions, and seek support when they encounter difficulties.

When belonging is missing, students may begin to withdraw from school experiences. They may avoid participation, struggle to develop peer relationships, or feel like they do not have a place within the community.

Building belonging requires schools to consider the complete student experience. Academic achievement matters, but so do the relationships and interactions that shape how students experience their environment.

Creating Belonging in Workplaces

Workplaces depend on belonging because employees contribute more than technical skills. They bring ideas, perspectives, creativity, and collaboration that influence organizational success.

When employees feel connected to their teams, they are more likely to communicate openly and contribute new ideas.

When they feel disconnected, they may limit their involvement and focus only on completing assigned responsibilities.

Leaders have a significant influence on whether belonging develops.

The way leaders respond to feedback, recognize contributions, handle challenges, and encourage participation communicates what kind of culture they are creating.

A workplace does not need everyone to think the same way or interact in the same manner. Strong cultures allow individuals to bring different perspectives while maintaining a shared sense of respect and connection.

Measuring Belonging Within an Organization

Belonging can sometimes seem difficult to evaluate because it is based on personal experience. However, organizations can examine the conditions that influence whether people feel connected.

Important questions include:

Do people feel recognized within this environment?

Do individuals have opportunities to contribute?

Are relationships supported alongside performance expectations?

Do people understand how they fit into the larger community?

These questions help organizations move beyond assumptions and gain a clearer understanding of their culture.

Creating Communities Where People Feel They Belong

Belonging is not created through a single initiative or occasional event. It develops through consistent experiences that communicate respect, recognition, and connection.

Schools and workplaces become stronger when people feel that they have a meaningful place within the communities around them.

They communicate more openly, participate more fully, and develop stronger relationships.

A healthy culture is not simply one where people are present. It is one where people understand that they matter.

Building belonging requires intentional effort, but the result is an environment where individuals are more connected to the people, purpose, and community around them.
