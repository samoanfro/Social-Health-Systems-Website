The 9-Module Curriculum

Six phases. Five Pillars. Nine observable, trainable, measurable capabilities.

How the Curriculum is Organized

The nine modules sequence into six phases that mirror how relational capability actually installs in adults. Each module installs one specific, observable capability. Each capability is assessed against a defined rubric at a defined credential tier.

Result: Participants don't "take" the curriculum in two days. They earn credentials in a multi-year arc with demonstrated behavior change, observed practice, and portfolio evidence.

How Modules Map to the Five Pillars

Each module installs capability aligned to one primary Pillar. The Pillars are the doctrine; the modules are how the doctrine becomes practice.

1. Connection Before Concern

People respond better to being understood than corrected. Modules 1, 5, 8.

2. Connection Currency Tools

Five foundational micro-behaviors that make big safety deposits. Modules 2, 3.

3. Statements Before Questions

Questions trigger defense; statements trigger reflection. Modules 4, 5.

4. Relational Response to Mental Health

Behavior is communication. Connection is intervention. Modules 6, 8.

5. Relationship-Based Culture

Curriculum doesn't teach. Relationships do. Modules 7, 9.

What Participants Leave With

Phase 1 — Foundation (Modules 1–4)

  • Precise definition of Social Health
  • First behavioral evidence of impact
  • Awareness of role in relational systems
  • Literacy in digital influence

Phase 2 — Communication Toolkit (Module 5)

  • Mastery of Communication Toolkit
  • Ability to de-escalate conflict
  • Capacity to deliver feedback that lands
  • Observed behavior change in 1:1s and team settings

Phase 3 — Self-Relational Work (Modules 6–7)

  • Interrupted self-criticism patterns
  • Self-relational awareness under stress
  • Deliberately designed peer network
  • Executive presence in high-stakes contexts

Phase 4 — Advanced Application (Module 8)

  • Mastery of negotiation under pressure
  • Full toolkit deployment in high-stakes contexts
  • Capacity to assess and support others
  • Authority to scale curriculum with fidelity
